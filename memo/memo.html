Strategy memo · Digital payments, Bangladesh

Payments are going digital. Mobile money is not, yet.

For MFS and bank leadership teamsFrom Md. ZubayerDate 15 Nov 2026Data Bangladesh Bank, FY15 to Jun 2026
The question, and the answer

Who wins digital payments in Bangladesh, and what should a mobile financial service (MFS) do next?

MFS still owns the mass market, but it is a cash rail, and the growth in payments has moved to bank apps. Internet banking grew 4.1 times in three years, to Tk 16.45 lakh crore in FY2025-26, close to MFS at Tk 21.56 lakh crore. Inside MFS, cash in and cash out grew from 58.5% to 60.6% of value. An MFS that wants to keep winning has to turn its cash rail into a payments network before bank apps reach its customers.

Recommendation for an MFS: three moves

1 · Pay, don't cash out

Merchant payment cheaper than cash-out

Merchant payments grew 124% in two years; cash-out grew 92% on a base eight times larger. Every taka cashed out leaves the network. Price and reward paying a shop above withdrawing cash.

2 · Capture money at source

Salary and remittance into the wallet

Salary grew 62% and inward remittance 191%. Money that arrives digitally needs no agent to cash it in, and is cheaper to keep in the network.

3 · Defend the base

Before bank apps go mass market

Internet banking customers doubled in one year and the average payment fell from Tk 78,372 to Tk 48,466. Banks are going retail. Add what customers want from a bank: savings, credit, transfers.

What I am asking for

  1. Set one target in value share, not users: merchant payments as a share of MFS value, reported monthly next to cash-out.
  2. Reprice the journey: make paying a merchant cheaper and more rewarding for the customer than cashing out at an agent.
  3. Sign the inflows: payroll with large employers and wallet credit with remittance houses, so money starts its life digital.

What the data says

1. Bank apps are growing fastest

Ten years ago internet banking handled Tk 0.18 lakh crore a year, an eighth of MFS. In FY2025-26 it handled Tk 16.45 lakh crore, three quarters of MFS. Most of that gap closed in the last three years: internet banking grew 4.1 times, MFS 1.8 times, cards 1.2 times, and agent banking barely moved. MFS growth is understated because Nagad did not report from March 2025 to February 2026, but the gap is far wider than Nagad's share could explain.

2. Inside MFS, cash still dominates

Bangladesh Bank's product detail runs from December 2021 to February 2025. Over that time the share of MFS value that is cash in or cash out did not fall. It rose, from 58.5% to 60.6%, because cash-out grew 92% in two years. Merchant payments grew faster, 124%, but only from 3.5% of value to 4.6%. Inward remittance nearly tripled, from a tiny base. MFS is still mostly how people move cash to each other, not how they pay.

What the data says

3. Banks are going retail

Internet banking customers doubled in FY2025-26, from 11.4 million to 22.7 million, and transactions rose from 78 million in FY2022-23 to 339 million. Meanwhile the average payment fell from Tk 78,372 in FY2023-24 to Tk 48,466. More payments, smaller payments: the pattern of retail customers, not only businesses. The average MFS transaction is Tk 2,250, so the two still serve different needs, but the overlap is growing.

4. The MFS market is a near-monopoly

In April to June 2026, bKash handled about 67% of MFS value, Nagad about 19% and Rocket about 14%; the top three handled 99.15%. The provider total, Tk 6.60 lakh crore, matches Bangladesh Bank's own monthly figures for the quarter within 0.4%. In a market this concentrated, the contest that matters is not between MFS providers. It is between MFS and the banks.

A data warning. Nagad did not report to Bangladesh Bank from March 2025 to February 2026. MFS agents fall 23% in March 2025 and rise 36% in March 2026, and MFS value jumps when Nagad returns. Any growth rate that crosses those months mixes market change with a reporting change. What would change my mind

  • Product detail after February 2025 showing merchant share jumping would mean MFS is already turning into a payments network.
  • Internet banking growth flattening in FY2026-27 would suggest FY2025-26 was large business transfers moving online, not retail.
  • Interoperability on equal terms between banks and MFS would blur the contest; the winner is then whoever owns the daily habit.

What this analysis cannot tell you

  • The mix after February 2025. Bangladesh Bank's site stops publishing MFS product detail there.
  • Retail versus business inside internet banking. The falling average suggests retail growth, but the split is not published.
  • Cash-out by merchants sits inside "cash out", so some merchant activity is hidden there.
  • Profitability. Fees and costs by product are not public, so the moves are framed by volume, not margin.
